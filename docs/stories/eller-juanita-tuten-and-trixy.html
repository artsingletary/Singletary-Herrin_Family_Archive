Eller Juanita Tuten and Trixy: Together Even in Death

Family History

Eller Juanita Tuten (1948–1958). AI-assisted restoration of a family photograph. Original photograph courtesy of Louie Loise Tuten Olsen.

Author: Family Archive Research

A Childhood in Miami

Eller Juanita Tuten was born in 1948. She was the granddaughter of Eller Matthews Mathis and the step-granddaughter of Glover Mathis. Contemporary newspaper accounts identify her father as Thomas Tuten and her mother as Mrs. Juanita Tuten. Family research suggests that her parents were Thomas Loise Tuten and Juanita Wells, although their identities require further verification. Her parents were divorced, and according to newspaper reports, Juanita had lived with her grandparents since birth at 8475 SW 4th Street in Miami, Florida. Juanita had cerebral palsy, a condition she had lived with since birth. According to her father, she attended school and experienced occasional attacks but did not suffer from paralysis. One of her closest companions was Trixy, a small terrier given to her by her uncle, William Tuten. The two shared a remarkable bond. William later told reporters, "The dog was her only companion. They were always together."

The Accident

On Wednesday, June 18, 1958, a tragic accident occurred at the Mathis family home in Miami. An article published in The Miami Herald the following day reported that Juanita's grandmother had left her alone for approximately ten minutes. When she returned with her husband, they discovered Juanita on her hands and knees on the living room floor, with her dress in flames. The sofa had also caught fire. Her grandparents cut away her burning clothing, wrapped her in a blanket, and rushed her to Jackson Memorial Hospital. Despite her injuries, Juanita was able to explain that she had ignited a wad of paper with a cigarette lighter and accidentally dropped it into her lap. She suffered third-degree burns covering approximately 70 percent of her body. Hospital officials described her condition as "very, very bad." Her father, Thomas Tuten, later told reporters that he and his wife had visited Juanita regularly and that she had received good care from her grandparents. He described the incident as a terrible accident.

Her Final Days

Juanita remained hospitalized in critical condition following the accident. During this time, neighbors cared for Trixy while she was separated from her beloved companion. Newspaper accounts described Juanita calling for Trixy from her hospital bed. On June 26, 1958, according to her cemetery memorial and several contemporary newspaper reports, Juanita died at Jackson Memorial Hospital in Miami. She was nine years old. Associated Press accounts reported that her final words were a call for Trixy. Even in her final moments, the little dog remained an important part of her life. These surviving accounts provide a glimpse of the extraordinary companionship that had existed between Juanita and Trixy throughout her childhood.

Together Even in Death

Shortly after Juanita's death, Trixy was struck and killed by an automobile. Associated Press reports placed the dog's death within hours of Juanita's passing, although other newspaper accounts differ regarding the precise timing. The unusual and sorrowful coincidence attracted widespread attention, with newspapers across Florida and beyond publishing accounts of their companionship. Juanita's funeral arrangements were handled by Ben Lanier & Son in Miami. She was buried at Woodlawn Park North Cemetery and Mausoleum, where her gravestone records the years 1948–1958. An Associated Press article published in the Tampa Bay Times on June 29, 1958, reported that she had been buried the previous day with a wreath of her favorite pink and yellow flowers resting on her small coffin. The family had considered burying Juanita and Trixy in the same grave but ultimately decided that the little dog should rest in the yard where the two had so often played together. Her uncle William Tuten buried Trixy behind the Mathis family home at 8475 SW 4th Street. According to newspaper reports, the family planned to place a small monument at the dog's resting place. Whether that monument was ever erected remains unknown.

Remembering Juanita

Although the newspaper coverage focused heavily on the circumstances of their deaths, the surviving accounts also preserve something of Juanita's life: a young girl who attended school, lived with her grandparents, loved pink and yellow flowers, and shared a close bond with a beloved companion. Her childhood portrait, gravestone, and contemporary newspaper reports preserve her place in the Mathis-Matthews-Tuten family history. A photograph published in the June 29, 1958, edition of the Tampa Bay Times provides another surviving visual record of Juanita. The historical address of her grandparents' Miami home connects her story to the place where she spent her childhood and where Trixy was ultimately laid to rest. Nearly seven decades later, the story of Juanita and Trixy remains a poignant chapter in the family's past, remembered not only for its tragic ending but also for the companionship they shared in life.

Sources and Research Notes

Contemporary newspaper coverage includes The Miami News, June 19 and June 29, 1958; The Miami Herald, June 19 and June 27, 1958; the Tallahassee Democrat, June 28, 1958; the Tampa Bay Times, June 29, 1958; The Ledger, June 29, 1958; and The Madison Courier, June 30, 1958. These accounts document Juanita's childhood, accident, hospitalization, death, funeral, family relationships, and the death and burial of her dog Trixy. Several newspapers published versions of the same Associated Press report and therefore do not necessarily represent independent corroboration. A newspaper article by Joseph Hoffman identifies her father as Thomas Tuten. Another identifies her mother as Mrs. Juanita Tuten. The identification of these individuals as Thomas Loise Tuten and Juanita Wells is based on family research and awaits additional documentation. Juanita's gravestone records 1948–1958, while her cemetery memorial identifies June 26, 1958, as her death date and Section 9A of Woodlawn Park North Cemetery as her burial location. The Tampa Bay Times reported that Juanita died Thursday morning, consistent with June 26, 1958. However, The Madison Courier and The Ledger reported her death at 12:15 a.m. Friday, which would correspond to June 27, 1958. The cemetery memorial date has been retained pending verification through an official death record. Newspaper accounts also differ concerning the timing of Trixy's death. Associated Press reports describe the dog dying within hours of Juanita's death, while the June 29 edition of The Miami News places the dog's death shortly before her funeral. That article also identifies the terrier as three years old and spells the dog's name Trixie rather than Trixy. The spelling Trixy has been retained throughout this story for consistency with the Associated Press accounts. The June 29 Tampa Bay Times report indicates that Juanita was buried on June 28, 1958. The existence and present whereabouts of the proposed monument for Trixy remain unverified.
