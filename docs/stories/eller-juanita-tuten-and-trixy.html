Eller Juanita Tuten and Trixy: Together Even in Death

Family History

Author: Family Archive Research

A Childhood in Miami

Eller Juanita Tuten was born in 1948. She was the granddaughter of Eller Matthews Mathis and the step-granddaughter of Glover Mathis. Contemporary newspaper accounts identify her father as Thomas Tuten and her mother as Mrs. Juanita Tuten. Family research suggests that her parents were Thomas Loise Tuten and Juanita Wells, although their identities require further verification. Her parents were divorced, and Juanita had lived with her grandparents since birth at 8475 SW 4th Street in Miami, Florida. Juanita had cerebral palsy, a condition she had lived with since birth. According to her father, she attended school and participated in everyday activities. One of her closest companions was Trixy, a small terrier who had been part of her life for several years. Newspaper accounts described the two as inseparable.

The Accident

On June 18, 1958, a tragic accident occurred at the Mathis family home in Miami. According to contemporary newspaper reports, Juanita had been playing with a cigarette lighter when she accidentally ignited a piece of paper. The flames spread to her clothing. When her grandparents discovered what had happened, they attempted to extinguish the flames and rushed her to Jackson Memorial Hospital. Juanita suffered extensive burns and remained hospitalized in critical condition. Her father, Thomas Tuten, later told reporters that he and his family had visited Juanita regularly and that she had received good care from her grandparents. He described the incident as a terrible accident.

Her Final Days

During her hospitalization, Juanita repeatedly asked for Trixy. The little dog had been placed in the care of neighbors while Juanita was in the hospital. On June 26, 1958, Juanita died at Jackson Memorial Hospital in Miami. She was nine years old. According to a June 27 article in The Miami Herald, her final words were a call for her beloved dog. The newspaper account provides a glimpse of the close companionship that had existed between Juanita and Trixy throughout her childhood.

Together Even in Death

Later that same morning, Trixy was struck and killed by an automobile. The deaths of Juanita and her beloved dog within hours of one another attracted widespread attention. Newspapers across Florida and beyond published accounts of the unusual and sorrowful coincidence. Juanita's uncle William Tuten described the two as constant companions. Juanita's funeral arrangements were handled by Ben Lanier & Son in Miami. She was buried at Woodlawn Park North Cemetery and Mausoleum in Miami, where her gravestone records the years 1948–1958. Trixy was buried behind the Mathis family home at 8475 SW 4th Street. According to newspaper reports, the family planned to place a small monument at the dog's resting place.

Remembering Juanita

Although the newspaper coverage focused heavily on the circumstances of their deaths, the surviving accounts also preserve something of Juanita's life: a young girl who attended school, lived with her grandparents, and shared a close bond with a beloved companion. Her childhood photograph, gravestone, and the contemporary newspaper reports preserve her place in the Mathis-Matthews-Tuten family history. The historical address of her grandparents' Miami home provides another connection to the places where the family lived. Nearly seven decades later, the story of Juanita and Trixy remains a poignant chapter in the family's past.

Sources and Research Notes

Contemporary newspaper coverage includes The Miami News, June 19, 1958; The Miami Herald, June 19 and June 27, 1958; the Tallahassee Democrat, June 28, 1958; and The Ledger, June 29, 1958. These accounts document Juanita's accident, hospitalization, death, family relationships, and the death of her dog Trixy. A newspaper article by Joseph Hoffman identifies her father as Thomas Tuten. Another identifies her mother as Mrs. Juanita Tuten. The identification of these individuals as Thomas Loise Tuten and Juanita Wells is based on family research and awaits additional documentation. Juanita's gravestone records 1948–1958, while her cemetery memorial identifies June 26, 1958, as her death date and Section 9A of Woodlawn Park North Cemetery as her burial location. Some newspaper reports differ in minor details, including the duration of Juanita's companionship with Trixy.
