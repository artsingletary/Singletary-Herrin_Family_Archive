A Fatal Encounter at Glenmore: Hiram Jackson Herrin, 1928 — Draft - Research in Progress

Family History

Author: Art Singletary

A Fatal Encounter

On Sunday, October 14, 1928, Hiram Jackson Herrin died in an exchange of gunfire near his home at Glenmore in Ware County, Georgia. He was 58 years old. The following day, The Macon Telegraph reported the incident under the headline "Death Climaxes Duel With Guns." Its account described a confrontation between Herrin, a local farmer and businessman, and F. W. Ivey, a turpentine operator, on the public highway in front of Herrin's home.

The Newspaper Account

According to The Macon Telegraph, officers reported that each man had fired one shot. Herrin died at the scene, while Ivey was taken to a hospital in Waycross with serious injuries. Although the newspaper described Ivey's condition as critical and predicted that he would not recover, the records collected so far do not establish his eventual fate.

What Led to the Shooting?

The newspaper reported that the men had been at odds over business matters. It also acknowledged that the immediate circumstances were unknown and that the two men were alone when the shooting occurred. Although the headlines called the encounter a duel, the available reports do not establish that it was formally arranged. Who initiated the confrontation, what was said, and the sequence of the shots remain unknown.

The Man Behind the Headline

Hiram was a son of William M. Herrin and Mary Hargraves. His gravestone records his birth as March 30, 1870, and his death certificate identifies his birthplace as Ware County, Georgia. On February 5, 1895, he married Nellie Ellington, later identified as Nellie Virginia Ellington Herrin. Their Ware County marriage record names him as H. J. Herring, reflecting a surname variation found in family records. By the time of his death, Hiram was living in Glenmore and working as a farmer. The newspaper also described him as a businessman.

Connecting the Records

Both supplied newspaper clippings identify the deceased as H. D. Herrin, while his death certificate names him as Hiram Jackson Herrin and his gravestone reads Hiram J. Herrin. Their matching death date, location, and circumstances strongly support the identification despite the conflicting initials. His death certificate records a fatal gunshot injury and gives his age as 58 years, 6 months, and 14 days. That stated age differs slightly from the age calculated from the March 30, 1870 birth date on his gravestone.

Burial and Family Remembrance

Hiram's death certificate records burial on October 15, 1928, at a place written as Indian Mount. His burial has been identified as Indian Mound Baptist Church Cemetery in Millwood, Ware County, Georgia. He rests in the Herrin Family Plot beside his wife, Nellie Virginia Ellington Herrin, whose gravestone records February 17, 1874 – September 30, 1960.

Questions for Further Research

This account remains a work in progress. Further newspaper coverage, an inquest, or court records may help explain the business disagreement, clarify the circumstances of the shooting, and establish what happened to F. W. Ivey. Additional research may also explain why the newspapers used the initials H. D. rather than H. J. Until further evidence is found, the newspaper's claims are attributed to its reporting rather than presented as independently established facts.

Sources and Research Notes

Sources include Hiram Jackson Herrin's Georgia death certificate, state file number 28071; the Ware County marriage record for H. J. Herring and Nellie Ellington, page 280; the photograph of Hiram and Nellie's graves; and the supplied newspaper clippings. The longer clipping is identified by its supplied filename as The Macon Telegraph, October 15, 1928. The shorter Associated Press clipping has a Waycross dateline of October 14, but its newspaper title and publication date have not yet been established.
