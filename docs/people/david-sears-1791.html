Biography

David Sears was born in North Carolina in 1791, the son of John Sears, a Revolutionary War soldier. According to Pioneers of Wiregrass Georgia, his father moved the family to Montgomery County, Georgia, around 1795 or 1796.

David married Levicy Nobles on April 19, 1810, in Montgomery County, Georgia. Levicy was the daughter of Lewis Sanders Nobles, who also served during the Revolutionary War.

David and Levicy raised thirteen children, including Charity Sears (1812–1898), who married Levi Herrin. Through Charity, they became ancestors of both branches of the Herrin family featured in the story Two Branches of the Herrin Family Reunite.

On June 20, 1809, David was commissioned an ensign in the 52nd District of Emanuel County. During the War of 1812, he served as a private in Captain John G. Colbert's company, 2nd Regiment of Georgia Militia. He enlisted on October 12, 1814, and was discharged on March 15, 1815, according to the published family history.

Around 1825, David moved his family from Montgomery County to what was then Ware County, Georgia, settling in the 586th Georgia Militia District. This territory subsequently became part of Clinch, Coffee, and Atkinson counties.

David Sears is named as a household head in the 1830 and 1840 census schedules attributed to Ware County, Georgia. These schedules name only household heads and count other residents by demographic categories. In 1840, his household included five males and three females in the free white population columns. The adult male and female were both aged 40–49, consistent with David's and Levicy's reported birth years. The unnamed residents cannot be individually identified from this record alone.

Their 1850 census household included Franklin Sears, age 19, younger David Sears, age 16, and an elderly man whose entry appears to read Lewis Nobles, age 90, born in South Carolina. He is provisionally identified as Levicy's father, Lewis Sanders Nobles. The census does not state relationships, and his name and age require further verification.

In the 1860 census, David and Levicy were recorded together in Coffee County, Georgia, aged 69 and 68. David's birthplace was North Carolina and Levicy's was Georgia. His personal estate was valued at $175. They were the only two people listed in their household.

In 1870, David was recorded in Coffee County as a schoolteacher, age 76, with $200 in personal estate. Lavina Sears, identified as his daughter, was listed with him, keeping house. Their recorded ages conflict with other family records: David's age differs from his reported 1791 birth year, while Lavina's age appears to be 27, substantially younger than either proposed birth year of 1814 or 1816.

The published family history references David in the 1820 Montgomery County census, the 1830, 1840, and 1850 Ware County censuses, and the 1860 Coffee County census.

David died in 1879. He and Levicy were buried at Union Hill Congregational Church cemetery, where they were members.

These genealogical and military claims are attributed to this compiled secondary source pending comparison with original records. The unannotated printed page gives Lavina's birth year as 1814; the earlier cropped copy contains a handwritten correction to 1816.
