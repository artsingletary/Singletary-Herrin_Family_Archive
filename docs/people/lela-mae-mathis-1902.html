Biography

Lela Mae Mathis Singletary was born on September 26, 1902, in Madison County, Florida, the daughter of Daniel M. Mathis and Mary Frances Darling. She grew up in a large family in rural North Florida. The 1910 United States Census records seven-year-old Lela living with her parents and siblings in the Norton Creek area of Madison County.

On March 6, 1917, Lela married George William Singletary in Lafayette County, Florida. Their marriage license was issued the previous day, March 5, and identifies her as Lela May Mathis. She was only fourteen years old at the time of her marriage, while George was twenty-one. George was working as a farmer in the Mayo area, and the couple began their married life during the years surrounding America's entry into World War I.

In June 1917, George registered for the military draft and reported that he was supporting his wife and one sister. He subsequently served in the United States Army from September 1917 until January 1918. During the early years of their marriage, Lela and George also provided a home for members of George's family. According to family history, George helped care for his younger sisters following the deaths of their parents, John Vershal Singletary and Mary Bryant Singletary. The exact dates of their deaths remain uncertain.

Lela and George's first documented child, Dewey Singletary, was born on August 23, 1919. An unusual detail appears in the 1920 United States Census, where Lela was recorded in two different households in Lafayette County. One enumeration lists seventeen-year-old Lela in the household of her parents, Daniel and Mary Mathis, alongside her siblings. Another, recorded on January 28, places her in the household of her husband George, together with their infant son Dewey and George's younger sisters Alice and Viola. The reason for this duplicate enumeration is unknown, but the records provide a valuable glimpse into Lela's continuing connections with both her birth family and her husband's family.

Over the following two decades, Lela and George raised a large family in Lafayette County. Their nine documented children were Dewey, Louie, Loyd McCoy, Irene Carol, William (Bill), Donald, John Vershal, Marlene, and Margaret Singletary. The 1930 census records Lela and George living in Election Precinct No. 3 with five children: Dewey, Louie, Loyd, Irene, and a young son recorded as George W., believed to be their son William. Lela's brother Glover Mathis was living nearby, illustrating the continued presence of her extended family in the area.

The family continued to grow during the 1930s. Donald had been born in October 1930, followed by John Vershal in 1933, Marlene in 1938, and Margaret in 1940. By the time Margaret was born, Lela was thirty-seven years old and had been married to George for more than twenty-three years. Although the surviving records provide limited information about her daily activities, they document her life within a large family spanning several generations of the Mathis, Darling, and Singletary families of North Florida.

Lela died on July 19, 1951, at her home near Mayo, Lafayette County, Florida. Her Florida death certificate identifies her parents as Daniel Mathis and Mary Darling and records acute gastritis as the cause of death. Her obituary, published in The Florida Times-Union on July 21, 1951, described her as a longtime Lafayette County resident and a member of Midway Baptist Church. Her funeral was held at Airline Baptist Church on July 20, 1951, and she was buried at Airline Cemetery. The obituary named her husband, G. W. Singletary, eight surviving children, four brothers—Glover, Roy, Rube, and Frank Mathis—and her sister Jessie Sapp. One son was named as Lonnie Singletary, possibly a newspaper error for Louie Singletary, although this identification has not been independently confirmed. The obituary reported her age as forty-six, consistent with her death certificate but differing from the age indicated by her recorded September 26, 1902, birth date.

An important discrepancy exists in the historical evidence concerning Lela's birth year. Her death certificate records September 26, 1905, but also gives her age as forty-six, two details that are inconsistent with one another. The 1910 census, which records her as seven years old, supports a birth year around 1902 or 1903. The family archive currently uses September 26, 1902, while preserving the conflicting information from her death certificate for future research.

Lela's life was closely connected to the rural communities of Madison and Lafayette counties. From her childhood in the Mathis household to her marriage to George Singletary and the raising of their nine documented children, her story reflects the interconnected families of North Florida during the first half of the twentieth century. Her descendants continued those family connections across Florida and beyond.
